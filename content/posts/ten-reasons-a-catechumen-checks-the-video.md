## The Video

The text from his brother came at 9:40 on a Tuesday night.

> Watch this before you do anything you can't undo. I love you. No joke.

Under it was a link. The title said *10 Reasons Not to Convert to Orthodoxy*.

Daniel was four months into the catechumen class at St. Nicholas. In six weeks he would be chrismated. He had already told his boss he'd need the Saturday off. He had already bought a small icon of St. John the Baptist for his desk, because that was the saint his priest had picked for him.

He almost didn't press play.

He pressed play.

The man in the video was friendly. He said he loved Orthodox people. He said he prayed for them. Then he went through ten reasons, calmly and quickly, and by the end Daniel had written ten numbers on a yellow legal pad and had a headache.

He had no answer for most of it. That was the part that scared him.

He texted Father Nicholas at 11:15 and felt silly doing it.

> Sorry for the late text. My brother sent me a video. I can't answer most of it. Is this normal?

The reply came before he put the phone down.

> Very normal. Don't answer anything tonight. Bring the pad Thursday. We'll take them one at a time. And Daniel, I don't know everything either. We'll find out together which things I know and which I only think I know.

Daniel looked at the pad. Next to each number he wrote one word.

*Icons. Atonement. Works. Mary. Tradition. Rules. Toll houses. Assurance. Sin. Exclusivity.*

Then, at the bottom, small: *What if he's right?*

## One: The Wall of Faces

Thursday evening the church was empty except for the candle stand and the smell of old wax.

Father Nicholas didn't start with an answer. He walked Daniel up the nave and stopped in front of the icon screen.

"He says this breaks the second commandment," Daniel said. "He read it out. 'You shall not make a carved image. You shall not bow down to them or serve them.'"

"Read it again. Slowly."

Daniel read it off his phone. "*You shall not bow down to them, nor serve them.*"

"Two verbs. In the Greek Old Testament, the Bible the apostles quoted, it's *proskynēsis* for bow and *latreia* for serve." Father Nicholas tapped the air. "Keep those two words. You'll need them tonight."

"He says bowing is the problem."

"Does he? He says God commanded carved cherubim for the Ark." Father Nicholas let that land. "In the same book. A few chapters later. He admits it in the video."

Daniel flipped back through his notes. He had written it down without noticing. *"There were God-prescribed images of cherubim."*

"If images were the sin, God commanded the sin. So the sin isn't images." Father Nicholas smiled. "And Joshua falls on his face before the Ark in chapter seven. Does Joshua commit idolatry?"

"No."

"So bowing isn't the sin either. Worshiping something that isn't God is the sin. Which is what the commandment says, if you read the whole sentence."

Daniel leaned on a pew. "He also said an early council banned images. Elvira. Canon 36."

"He's right that the canon exists. The Latin says, roughly, that what is worshiped and adored shouldn't be painted on the walls." Father Nicholas shrugged. "It was a small synod in Spain, around the year 300. Scholars still argue about what it meant and why. I wouldn't hang a whole doctrine on it, and he wouldn't hang the Reformation on a small Spanish council if it said something he disliked."

"And Nicaea II? He said it damns people who don't kiss icons."

"Read what it says."

Daniel had printed it. The council's own text, not a blog. He read: "*The honour which is paid to the image passes on to that which the image represents.* And then: *not indeed that true worship of faith, latreia, which pertains alone to the divine nature.*"

"There's your second word," Father Nicholas said. "Honor goes through the image to the person. Adoration is for God alone. That isn't a footnote. That's the definition."

"But he's right that it says anathema." Daniel's voice dropped. "On people who won't salute the images. That part is true. I read it."

"It is true." Father Nicholas didn't flinch. "I won't tell you it's not there. What exactly an anathema meant in the year 787, whether it meant being put out of communion or something worse, scholars and priests still talk about. I can tell you what I was taught. I won't pretend it's simpler than it is. And look at the other anathema in the same text. 'If anyone does not confess that Christ our God can be represented in his humanity, let him be anathema.' That's what the whole fight was about. Can the Son of God have a face?"

Daniel looked at the icon of Christ above the doors. It was an old, plain painting. The eyes seemed to look at him and past him at the same time.

*God became visible. So His face can be painted.*

On the pad he wrote one line next to *Icons*:

> *Two verbs. Bow isn't serve. And the fight was really about whether God has a face.*

Then, under it, small: *Ask Father: what does "anathema" mean here?*

## Two: The Verse That Isn't There

Daniel didn't sleep well that week.

He thought atonement was the one that mattered. The man in the video had been so sure. *God's justice needs to be satisfied. Either we take the penalty in hell or Jesus takes it. There is no third option.* Daniel had grown up on a version of that. A hymn from his childhood church still sat in his head. *In my place condemned he stood.*

He met Father Nicholas on Saturday morning at the parish library, a room with one table and a very old coffee machine.

"He used Isaiah," Daniel said. "Chapter 53, verse 10. 'It was the will of the Lord to crush him.' That's the one the whole thing rests on."

"Which Bible was he reading?"

"I don't know. It was on the screen."

"Probably a modern English one, built from the Hebrew text." Father Nicholas pulled a thick Orthodox Study Bible off the shelf. "Our Old Testament comes from the Greek, the Septuagint. It's the one the apostles quote. Look."

Daniel read it twice. Then a third time, because he didn't trust himself.

*The Lord also is pleased to purge him from his stroke.*

"Purge," Daniel said. "Not crush."

"Not crush."

"But isn't that just a different translation of the same thing?"

"It's a different text. The Hebrew-based reading and the Greek reading really do differ here." Father Nicholas sat down. "I want to be careful, Daniel. I'm not telling you Isaiah doesn't say Christ bore our sins. The Church reads Isaiah 53 as Christ bearing our sins. All of it. The question isn't whether He bore them. The question is what *kind* of thing the Cross is."

"What kind is it?"

"The Father didn't beat the Son. The Father, the Son and the Spirit act as one. The Son goes into death on purpose, the way a doctor goes into a plague house." He nodded at the Bible. "Hebrews says He took on flesh and blood 'that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death.' It's rescue. The man in the video is right that sin is serious and the Cross cost something. He's protecting something true. He just thinks the courtroom is the whole picture."

"You're saying it's a hospital."

"I'm saying the Church has always called it both. It leans on the hospital." Father Nicholas smiled. "And I'm saying that when someone says 'the Bible says' and puts a verse on the screen, it's fair to ask which Bible."

Daniel wrote:

> *Isa 53:10 in the Greek: "pleased to purge him from his stroke." Ask which Bible. And the Cross is rescue, not a beating.*

He drew a small star next to it.

## Three: The Apple

Marcus picked the diner. He always did.

His brother had driven two hours. He wasn't angry, which made it worse. Marcus was a deacon at his church and had the gentle, worried face of someone who loved you and thought you were walking into a river.

"So," Marcus said. "Did you watch it?"

"I watched it."

"And?"

"I'm working through it." Daniel stirred his coffee. "I wanted to start with the one I think you care about most. Faith and works."

Marcus nodded slowly. "That one's the gospel, Dan. It's the whole thing. 'By grace you have been saved through faith. Not by works, so that no one may boast.' You can't add anything to that."

"I agree with that verse."

"Then how can you join a church that says it's faith *and* works?"

Daniel took out the printout. "He quoted a document called the Confession of Dositheus. It's from 1672. I looked it up myself." He read it aloud. "*We believe a man to be not simply justified through faith alone, but through faith which works through love, that is to say, through faith and works.*"

"See?" Marcus said, quietly. "There it is."

"He quoted it accurately. I'm not going to say he didn't." Daniel took a breath. "But look at what it says. Faith *which works through love*. That's Paul. Galatians 5:6. 'Faith working through love.' Paul's own summary. It isn't two things added up. It's one living thing."

Marcus frowned. "Ephesians 2 says works aren't how you're saved."

"Verses 8 and 9. And what's verse 10?"

Marcus opened his mouth, then closed it. Daniel watched him run through it in his head. He knew his brother's Bible better than he did.

"'For we are His workmanship,'" Marcus said slowly, "'created in Christ Jesus for good works.'"

"The video stops at verse 9."

"It's a *video*, Dan. It's ten minutes."

"I know. I'm not mad at him." Daniel pulled out another sheet. "James 2:24 says, 'You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone.' Which, as far as I can tell, is the only place in the whole New Testament where the words 'faith alone' show up. And it says *not*."

Marcus didn't say anything for a while.

"That's James," he said finally. "He means works show the faith is real."

"Maybe. That's a fair reading. I'm not saying you're being silly." Daniel hesitated, then said the thing that had been sitting in him all week. "You know what I think the real worry is? Nobody wants to be the guy who thinks he can *earn* it. I grew up scared of that. Nobody should boast. And I think the Church knows that too. It's not about earning. It's about not being the kind of person whose faith is just a word."

Marcus looked at him for a long moment.

"The apple thing," he said. "A free gift can't require work."

"If you hand me an apple and I reach out my hand, did I earn it?"

"No."

"Then I think we agree more than the video lets on."

They finished the pie. Marcus paid.

On the pad, that night, Daniel wrote:

> *Dositheus said it. Quote is real. But "faith working through love" is Paul's own phrase. And the video stops at Eph 2:9; verse 10 says works are the purpose, not the wage.*

## Four: The Hymn

The Mary section was the one Daniel dreaded.

He'd been uneasy about it from the start. At his old church Mary appeared once a year, at Christmas, and then went back into the cupboard. The man in the video had read out lines from the Akathist in a mocking voice. *You are the only hope of the hopeless. Deliverer from the mire of sin.* And then, in the same voice: *No. Jesus is not Mary.*

Daniel had felt his stomach drop.

"So here's what I did," he told Father Nicholas on Thursday. "I tried to find those lines in the Akathist. Two of them, I couldn't match. The one about salvation by chanting hymns of praise, and 'only hope of the hopeless.' Maybe I'm looking in the wrong translation. But I want to see the actual text before I react to a clip."

"That's the right thing to do," Father Nicholas said. "Most people never check."

"He's also saying we teach all kinds of things about her. That she never sinned. That she's ever-virgin. That her body was taken to heaven. And titles. Queen of Heaven. Mediatrix."

Father Nicholas sat down and was quiet for a bit. Daniel noticed it. He seemed to be choosing his words.

"Let me sort it into piles. Some of this is Rome, not us. The Immaculate Conception, the idea that Mary was kept free from the stain of original sin at her conception, is a Roman Catholic teaching. We don't hold it. We don't call her a co-redeemer either. 'Mediatrix' is a Latin title. We say there is one Mediator."

"He lumped them together."

"A lot of people do. It's an easy mistake." He raised a finger. "Second pile. Some of it is how we *pray*, not what we define. The hymns are praise poetry. They're written the way praise is written. If I sing to my wife that she's the light of my life, nobody thinks I've announced a theory of astronomy."

Daniel laughed despite himself.

"And a lot of it is grammar. 'Through her.' Look at what Irenaeus wrote, in the second century. I've got the passage." He found the page. "*Mary... having become the cause of salvation, both to herself and the whole human race.* The cause. Because through her, Christ came."

"That's a real quote?"

"*Against Heresies*, Book 3, chapter 22. I checked it myself." Father Nicholas tapped the page. "'Cause of salvation' is the same grammar as the hymns. It doesn't say she *is* the Savior. It says through her, the Savior came. Paul says of himself, 'I have become all things to all men that I may by all means save some.' Nobody thinks Paul is a second savior."

"What about the rest?" Daniel asked. "The Dormition. Her body in heaven. He says there's no Scripture for it."

"He's right. There isn't."

Daniel blinked.

"The Dormition is something we know from the Church's life and worship, not from a verse. I'm not going to hide that. And I'll be honest with you." Father Nicholas looked at him steadily. "Some of what we say about Mary is firm teaching, and some is the Church's reverent opinion about things Scripture doesn't tell us. I'm happy to walk you through which is which. But I'd rather do it carefully, over a few weeks, than give you a fast answer so you feel better tonight."

Daniel thought about that. It was oddly comforting. It was the first time anyone in the whole discussion had said *there's something here I won't pretend about.*

He wrote:

> *Immaculate Conception, "co-redeemer," "mediatrix" = Rome, not us. Hymns are praise grammar. Irenaeus III.22.4: "cause of salvation." Find the real Akathist text before reacting. Ask Father: what's firm, what's opinion.*

## Five: The Teacher and His Book

"He used a story," Daniel told Marcus on the phone. "About a teacher who wrote a book. Years after he's gone, people say you can't understand the book unless you come to them. And the speaker says, 'No. Just read the book.'"

"It's a good point," Marcus said.

"It is. It's a good picture." Daniel was pacing. "Let me flip it. Imagine a teacher who spent months with a group of people. Daily. In person. Then he writes them two short letters. Do those two letters hold everything he taught?"

"No. Of course not."

"That's Paul and the Thessalonians. And look what Paul writes to them." He read it off his phone. "*Hold to the traditions which you were taught, whether by word of mouth or by letter from us.*"

"I know that verse."

"The video quotes it. The speaker read it out loud. And then he said the only way to know which traditions are apostolic is to check them against Scripture. But Paul doesn't say that. Paul says 'word of mouth *or* letter.' Same weight."

Marcus was quiet. Then, carefully, "That's a fair catch. But the Church can't just say anything is tradition."

"I agree. That's why the question is who decides." Daniel pushed on. "Acts 15. The first big church argument. Gentiles and circumcision. And how does it end? 'It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us.' The apostles and elders decide. As a group. With the Spirit."

"That's the apostles, though."

"It is. But the letter from that meeting got carried to the churches as binding." He took a breath. "Can I ask you something? Who decided which books are in the New Testament?"

Marcus laughed, slowly. "The church. Over a few hundred years."

"And the list of the twenty-seven books isn't in the twenty-seven books."

"No."

"So 'Scripture alone' sits on a list that Scripture doesn't contain." Daniel stopped pacing. "I'm not saying this to win. I just wanted you to hear it from me. I'm not leaving the Bible, Marcus. I'm trying to read it inside the people who wrote it down."

There was a long silence on the line.

"I don't love that I can't answer it quickly," Marcus said at last.

"I couldn't answer any of yours quickly either."

They both laughed.

> *2 Thess 2:15 = spoken and written, same weight. Acts 15:28. The list of books isn't in the books.*

## Six: The Rules

Daniel came to the next meeting a little sheepish.

"I need to ask you something that might be rude."

"Ask," Father Nicholas said.

"The video says Orthodox let priests marry but not bishops. And I realized I've been calling you Father for four months and never asked." He fidgeted. "Are you married?"

Father Nicholas laughed so hard he had to put down his coffee. "Presbytera Anna would love that. Yes. Twenty-two years. Four kids. Come to dinner Sunday."

"So priests *can* marry."

"Married men are ordained priests and deacons all the time. That's normal for us." He wiped his eyes. "Here's the thing about that claim. He's half right and he points the wrong way. In the year 692 a council met, called Trullo. Canon 13 says, and I'm reading it now, that it was *handed down as a rule of the Roman Church* that clergy promise not to live with their wives. And the council says, no, the Eastern way is, *the lawful marriages of men who are in holy orders* are to be firm. They rebuked Rome for the very thing the video says is wrong."

"So the thing he's against is Roman."

"On this point, yes."

"And bishops?"

"Bishops are chosen from monks and widowers. That's true, and he's right about the practice. Canon 12, same council, says bishops shouldn't live with wives after they're consecrated. It's a *discipline*. It's not a teaching that marriage is bad. We call marriage a sacrament. Paul's phrase in First Timothy is about people who forbid marriage as *evil*. We don't."

"1 Timothy 3 says a bishop should be the husband of one wife."

"One. It's a ceiling on the number. It doesn't tell every bishop he has to marry. Paul himself wasn't married. He says so, plainly, in First Corinthians."

Daniel scribbled. "And the fasting before communion. He said Paul tells people to eat at home first."

"Look at First Corinthians 11 with me." Father Nicholas found the page. "Some people at Corinth were treating the Lord's Supper like a dinner party. The rich ate first. Some got drunk. Paul says, if you're that hungry, eat at home, so the Lord's table isn't a meal. He's *separating* the dinner from the sacrament. He isn't telling anyone to eat breakfast before communion."

"And you fast anyway."

"It's an old discipline. A council in North Africa, at Hippo, in 393, says the sacrament isn't celebrated except by people who are fasting. It's old. It isn't a commandment in Paul. I won't pretend it is. It's ancient practice, kept out of reverence." He smiled. "I'll also say that a rule can become a habit without the heart. Christ warned about that, too. The man in the video is right to be wary of it."

Daniel wrote it down and didn't say anything for a moment.

"I thought these would be the hard ones," he said.

"They sound hard because they sound like rules. They turn out to be the easy ones."

> *Trullo c.13: the council defends married priests against Rome. Trullo c.12: bishop rule is discipline. 1 Tim 3:2 is a cap, not an order. 1 Cor 11 separates meal from sacrament. Fast attested at Hippo, 393.*

## Seven: The Part Father Didn't Defend

"I don't want to do toll houses," Daniel said.

They were in the church hall after Vespers, folding chairs, a tray of cookies nobody was eating.

"The video says there are checkpoints after you die. Demons accuse you of your sins. It's absent from the Bible. And it destroys assurance. I looked it up and the more I read, the more it sounded like a horror story."

Father Nicholas sat down heavily. He didn't look pleased. He didn't look defensive either.

"I'll tell you what I think and what I don't. I don't need toll houses. A lot of Orthodox don't. It's a teaching some saints and some stories hold, and the Church has never made it a dogma. It's what we call a *theologoumenon*, a theological opinion. Some Orthodox speak of it as a vivid way to describe the soul's reckoning. Some hold it more literally. Others warn that it has been used to scare people. Faithful Orthodox disagree about it."

"So I'm not required to believe it?"

"You're not required to believe it. I'm not sure it's any priest's job to impose it." He hesitated. "I'd say the same about a few other things you'll hear. I'd rather say 'this is an opinion' than defend it because someone told me it was ours."

Daniel pulled out his notes. "He also said prayers for the dead contradict Luke 16. The chasm. And he quoted something from Dositheus. The same document as before."

"Read it."

Daniel read it from the printout. "*The souls of those involved in mortal sins depart into Hades, and there endure the punishment due to the sins they have committed... and are delivered by the Supreme Goodness, through the prayers of the Priests, and the good works which the relatives of each do for their Departed; especially the unbloody Sacrifice.*"

"That's accurate as far as it goes."

"It's not the whole sentence." Daniel slid the second page across. "It starts earlier. It says this is about people who *repented* before they died. They hadn't borne the fruit yet. They hadn't made it right. The video skips that part."

Father Nicholas looked at the page for a long moment. "So the video quotes a sentence about people who repented, and presents it like it applies to anyone."

"I don't think he meant to cheat. I think he read it somewhere and trusted it." Daniel exhaled. "But it changes what it says."

"It does. And it's a seventeenth-century local council. Not an ecumenical one. Worth knowing." Father Nicholas leaned back. "Luke 16 is about a man who never repented. Nobody prays that he'll be moved. The Church prays for her own, the repentant, because we're one Body. Death doesn't dissolve the Body. 'God is the God of the living.'"

Daniel thought about his grandmother. She'd died when he was nineteen. His old church had no way to hold that. He'd just stood at the back during the funeral and felt like the end of a sentence.

"There's a service at the end of the month for the departed," Father Nicholas said, as if reading his mind. "You can write her name on the list."

Daniel didn't trust his voice.

> *Toll houses = a theological opinion, not dogma. Dositheus 18 is about the repentant, and the video drops that clause. Prayers for the dead come from one Body (Matt 22:32).*

He wrote one more line and underlined it twice: *Ask Gran's name for the list.*

## Eight: The Fear

Marcus called on a Sunday night.

"The assurance thing," he said. "I keep coming back to it. It's the one that bothers me the most. Because I've always had that. I've always been able to say, 'I know where I'm going.' And you can't say that. Your church says you can't."

Daniel sat on the kitchen floor with his back against the cabinet.

"I'll tell you the truth," he said. "I don't have that peace the way you do. I'm not sure I ever did."

"What do you mean?"

"When I was a kid I'd pray the prayer at the altar call. Then again the next summer, in case it hadn't taken. Then again in college. I was never sure the *real one* had happened." He rubbed his eyes. "I'm not telling you that to win. I'm telling you because I think I'd been anxious for years under the label of assurance."

"That's not what assurance is supposed to be."

"I know. I'm not saying it's what you teach. I'm saying it's what happened to me." Daniel took a breath. "Can I read you something? Paul, in First Corinthians 9. *I discipline my body and keep it under control, lest after preaching to others I myself should be disqualified.* Paul."

"Paul's not afraid of hell."

"I don't know what Paul's afraid of. I know he wrote that. And in chapter 10: *Let anyone who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall.* And Hebrews warns people who have been enlightened and tasted the heavenly gift." Daniel swallowed. "And Jesus said 'the one who endures to the end will be saved.'"

"That's about..." Marcus stopped.

"I know it's contested. I'm not saying I'm right. I'm saying I can't un-read it." He softened. "I trust Christ's promise completely. What I don't trust is *me*. I could walk away. People do. And the Church tells me to say the Publican's prayer. 'God, be merciful to me, a sinner.' And honestly, it's the first prayer that's ever felt like rest."

The line went quiet.

"That's not what I expected you to say," Marcus said.

"It isn't what I expected either."

"I'm still not convinced."

"You don't have to be."

After they hung up Daniel sat on the kitchen floor for a long time. He wasn't certain of anything about himself. Oddly, that felt lighter than it had in years.

> *Trust God's promise completely. Don't claim a guarantee about me never leaving. 1 Cor 9:27, 10:12, Matt 24:13. The Publican's prayer, Luke 18:13.*

## Nine: The Preposition

Reading Greek wasn't Daniel's skill, so he went to the library again.

He found a commentary, then another, and read for two hours. By the end his head hurt in a good way.

He brought it to Father Nicholas on Thursday.

"I want to ask about original sin. The video says Adam represented all of us. We inherit his guilt. Romans 5:12, 'death spread to all men because all sinned.' And David says, 'in sin did my mother conceive me.' And that Orthodox deny this."

"We say it differently," Father Nicholas said. "We say ancestral sin. Adam's fall wounded the whole human race. We inherit death, corruption, a bent toward sin. But each person is guilty of his own sins, not Adam's."

"I found the Greek," Daniel said. "The phrase in Romans 5:12 is *eph' hō*. The Latin Bible says *in quo*, 'in whom all sinned.' The Greek reads more like 'because all sinned.'"

"That's a famous crux."

"Right, but I also found that the story people tell, that Augustine got it wrong over one preposition, is too simple." He read it off a page. "An earlier writer, Ambrosiaster, read the Latin that way before Augustine. And Augustine used other texts too. Psalm 51. Job 14. First Corinthians 15. And there's a scholar, Fitzmyer, who argues the phrase means something like 'with the result that,' neither of the two camps."

Father Nicholas raised his eyebrows. "You did your homework."

"I wanted to see if I'd been sold a slogan."

"And?"

"I think a slogan was sold. In both directions." Daniel set the page down. "I think the honest thing is to say it's a real debate, not a settled one. What I can say with confidence is Ezekiel 18:20. 'The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father.' That's not a loose phrase. That's the prophet."

"That's good. I'd keep it exactly that modest." Father Nicholas nodded slowly. "Don't let anyone tell you the Greek Fathers are all on one side and Augustine's on the other. That's a book-length question. If you want to speak with confidence, read them yourself first. I'd be glad to read them with you."

"And the hitman?"

"What hitman?"

"The video compares Adam to someone who hires a hitman. He acts for you, so you're guilty." Daniel frowned. "But Adam never hired anybody. We weren't born yet."

Father Nicholas laughed. "That analogy has a lot of problems. I'd leave it alone."

"He also says Orthodoxy denies sin is universal. And that's not true. We agree everyone is wounded. The argument is only about guilt."

"Right. And that's why I'd say we're closer to Marcus than the video lets on. We both think the whole human race is broken and needs Christ. The question is what *kind* of brokenness, and what Christ does about it."

> *Ancestral sin: death, corruption, bent. Each person guilty of his own. Ezek 18:20. Rom 5:12's "eph' hō" is genuinely debated. Don't claim "Augustine mistranslated." Read the sources first.*

## Ten: Where the Church Is

The last point scared him the most, because it was the one about people he loved.

*Exclusivity.* The man in the video said Orthodox claim you have to be in their church to be saved. He quoted an archbishop. Daniel couldn't find the quote anywhere. Then the man said the real Church is all genuine believers, and the right test of a church is whether it follows the Bible.

On the drive to the church Daniel realized he'd been dreading it since the first night. Because if it was true, then Marcus was lost. And their mother. And a hundred people he'd loved in a little brick church with a gym and a coffee urn.

He sat in the parking lot for ten minutes before going in.

"Father, I need to ask something and I'm scared of the answer."

"Ask."

"Does the Orthodox Church say Marcus is going to hell?"

Father Nicholas didn't answer right away. He walked to a side table and picked up a battered paperback.

"There's a bishop named Kallistos Ware. A theologian. English, a convert, wrote a famous book on the Orthodox Church. He wrote a line that a lot of Orthodox repeat." He read it from the page. "*We know where the Church is but we cannot be sure where it is not.*"

"Is that official?"

"No. I need to be honest. Some Orthodox think it's too loose. Others think it's exactly right. It's one bishop's way of putting a cautious position. I'm giving it to you as that, not as a definition." He closed the book. "Here's what I can say for certain. The Church isn't in the business of handing out verdicts about people. That's God's. I don't know where Marcus stands with Christ. Neither does the man in the video. And the Church doesn't claim to."

Daniel breathed out. His shoulders came down an inch.

"But the Church does say it's the one Body. One body, one baptism. 'Is Christ divided?' In Matthew 18, 'tell it to the church.' That's a thing you can point to. A body that can hear a complaint and decide. And Acts says believers were *added*."

"He says the real church is all believers, everywhere, no matter where they worship."

"A lot of Protestants say that and mean it kindly." Father Nicholas set the paperback down. "I'd only ask one thing. How do you tell which believers are genuine? And who decides which church is faithful to the Bible? The man's test, 'is it consistent with Scripture?', asks every seeker to be the final judge of every argument. That's the same anxiety he says the Orthodox cause. It's just moved."

Daniel thought about all those altar calls.

"And Iglesia ni Cristo, the other group that says it's the only church?"

"Anyone can claim anything. A claim isn't evidence. The question is which claim is *true*. For us that's continuity from the apostles, the bishops, the worship, the whole life. You can examine it. You can test it. You've been testing it for ten weeks."

Daniel nodded slowly. He was quiet for a while.

"There's one more thing," Father Nicholas said. "You asked if this is scary. It should be a little. Joining the Church is a serious step. If you ever want to step back, you can. I won't think less of you. But I'd hate for you to leave because of a quote nobody can find."

> *Ware's line: "We know where the Church is but we cannot be sure where it is not." Contested among Orthodox, so present it as a cautious position, not a definition. A claim isn't evidence. His test just moves the anxiety.*

## The Letter

Daniel wrote to Marcus the night before his chrismation. He wrote it by hand, on the yellow pad, because typing felt too clean.

> Marcus,
>
> I'm not writing to win. I want you to know exactly what I did and why.
>
> I watched your video. I didn't have answers. So I checked every claim against the actual text, one at a time, over ten weeks.
>
> Some of it was true. The video was right that the Council of Nicaea II put an anathema on people who won't salute the images. It was right that Dositheus says "faith and works." It was right that bishops are chosen from the celibate. I didn't hide from any of that.
>
> Some of it was off. Isaiah 53:10 in the Greek Old Testament doesn't say "crush." The Dositheus quote leaves out a clause. The Council of Elvira was a small Spanish synod. The council that rules on married priests defends them against Rome. Some quotes had no source at all.
>
> And some of it I still can't fully answer. I asked Father about those. He told me which parts are firm and which are opinion. That honesty did more for me than any argument.
>
> I'm not leaving Christ. I'm trying to be closer to the people who first wrote Him down.
>
> I love you. I'm not afraid of you. I'd like to keep arguing for the next forty years.
>
> Daniel
>
> P.S. You're invited Saturday. There's a coffee hour after. It's terrible coffee.

Marcus didn't answer for three days.

Then a text came in, one line.

> I'll bring my own coffee.

Daniel put his phone down and looked at the icon of St. John the Baptist on his desk. The old saint looked back with a long, unhurried face.

He turned to the legal pad, flipped to a clean page, and wrote at the top: *Questions for next week.* Then, under it:

*1. What's firm and what's opinion about Mary?*
*2. What did "anathema" mean at Nicaea II?*
*3. Ask Marcus what he thinks the Church is.*

He stopped there and smiled. That last one was the real question. He'd been so busy answering that he hadn't asked.
