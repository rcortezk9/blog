Summary

A tiered, curated reading list (hub note) for building Orthodox understanding of how the Church Fathers read Scripture and how doctrine formed alongside that exegesis. It moves deliberately from method-orienting overviews → hermeneutical argument → primary patristic sources → doctrinal/conciliar history, so the reader grasps patristic method before meeting the Fathers cold. The center of gravity is the relationship between exegesis (typology, theoria, letter vs. spirit) and the formation of dogma pre- and post-Nicaea.

Key Points

  • Tier 1 — Foundational (method first): John Breck, Scripture in Tradition (the recommended starting point: typology, theoria, letter vs. spirit); O'Keefe & Reno, Sanctified Vision (best short textbook on Antiochene vs. Alexandrian schools); Florovsky, Bible, Church, Tradition (founding essay for the modern Orthodox framing of the Scripture/Tradition relationship).
  • Tier 2 — Hermeneutics as a discipline (the theological argument): Andrew Louth, Discerning the Mystery (allegory/typology as theologically serious, strong for apologetics); John Behr, John the Theologian and His Paschal Gospel (Pascha, not historical reconstruction, as the Fathers' hermeneutical key); Frances Young, Biblical Exegesis and the Formation of Christian Culture (rigorous non-Orthodox companion/check).
  • Tier 3 — Primary sources (the method in practice): Chrysostom (Homilies on Genesis / Matthew / John — Antiochene "plain sense"); Gregory of Nyssa (Life of Moses — the paradigm of spiritual/allegorical ascent); Maximus the Confessor (Questions to Thalassius — typology at its most theologically loaded); Basil (Hexaemeron — creation); Origen (Homilies on Genesis and Exodus — method-shaping for all who followed, even where his speculation was later condemned).
  • Tier 4 — Doctrinal formation / church history: Behr's The Way to Nicaea and The Nicene Faith (how doctrine developed out of scriptural exegesis); Meyendorff, Byzantine Theology; Leo Donald Davis, The First Seven Ecumenical Councils.
  • Tier 0 — Optional single-volume overview: McGuckin, The Orthodox Church (history + doctrine + spiritual culture in one book; "meatier than Ware").
  • Sourcing tip: Holy Trinity Publications (Jordanville) often carries both primary texts and Orthodox secondary literature cheaper than academic-press editions, especially for the Tier 3 sources.

Details

The list's pedagogical logic is its most useful feature: it refuses to drop the reader into primary sources without first installing the interpretive grammar. Tiers 1–2 are about patristic reading (typology, theoria, the Antiochene/Alexandrian distinction, the case that allegory is theologically serious rather than primitive); Tier 3 is the Fathers actually doing it; Tier 4 shows exegesis and dogma co-developing through the councils.

Two threads connect this directly to existing vault material. First, Behr's two-volume Formation of Christian Theology is flagged as the single best cross-reference for the Filioque/Creed work and pairs with the Nicaea material already synthesized in the wiki — the claim that Nicene dogma grew out of scriptural exegesis (not over against it) is the same point made by source_uoc_09_trinity_nicaea_325. Second, Florovsky's Bible, Church, Tradition is the modern Orthodox anchor for the Scripture/Tradition relationship, which is exactly the terrain of comparison_sola_scriptura_orthodox_critique — the reading list supplies the constructive (how the Fathers did read) counterpart to that page's critique of Sola Scriptura.

The note also carries forward integration ideas: each Tier 3 primary source can spawn its own corpus note as it is read (e.g. life_of_moses.md, questions_to_thalassius.md), and an /apologetics-gap pass after 2–3 books would surface where this reading intersects existing vault gaps (Filioque, soteriology, the Israel-and-the-Church series).
