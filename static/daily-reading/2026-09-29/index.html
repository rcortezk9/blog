Luke, chapter five, verses twelve through sixteen.
12And it happened when He was in a certain city, that behold, a man who was full of leprosy saw Jesus; and he fell on his face and implored Him, saying, Lord, if You are willing, You can make me clean. 13Then He put out His hand and touched him, saying, I am willing; be cleansed. Immediately the leprosy left him. 14And He charged him to tell no one, But go and show yourself to the priest, and make an offering for your cleansing, as a testimony to them, just as Moses commanded. 15However, the report went around concerning Him all the more; and great multitudes came together to hear, and to be healed by Him of their infirmities. 16So He Himself often withdrew into the wilderness and prayed.

Daily Formation Synthesis.

What is the Church teaching your soul today?

Today the Church calls you to stop waiting for the people and the parts of yourself that disgust you to clean up before you love them. Christ did not wait for the leper's skin to heal before He touched him — the touch was the healing, willed before it was warranted. That is the love Ephesians asks of every husband, and it is the love asked of you in every relationship where you are tempted to hold back until the other person deserves your nearness. Fall down like the leper if you must; the Lord's answer has already been given: "I am willing."

Ascetic posture for today: Move toward the person or the confession you've been keeping at arm's length, before it becomes presentable — the willingness, not the outcome, is what love requires of you first.

From the Orthodox Study Bible

Galatians 5:22✠ This passage is read on days commemorating holy monks.
Galatians 5:25A walk in the Spirit comes from life in the Spirit.
Galatians 6:1To be able to administer correction with gentleness is a spiritual gift.
Galatians 6:2Paul admonishes us to bear one another’s burdens. But moments later, he notes that “each one shall bear his own load” (v. 5). Both are true. We are called to initiate caregiving in the Church, and not to be needless burdens to others.
Luke 5:12Leprosy was one of the most dreaded diseases of the time. It brought great physical suffering as well as total banishment and isolation from society. It is also a symbol of our sin.
Luke 5:14Show yourself to the priest: Christ gives this command in order to convince the priests by a tangible miracle that He is superior to Moses (CyrAl). The priests hold Moses to be greater than Christ, yet Christ heals a leper immediately and with His own divine authority. However, when Miriam was struck with leprosy, Moses had to seek mercy from above, and still she was only healed after seven days (Nm 12:10–15).
